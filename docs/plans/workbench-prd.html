Executive summary

ScalaFIM Workbench is where ScalaFIM becomes explorable: a desktop scientific thinking environment that keeps fMRI data, spatial selections, models, analyses, and results connected. Its first compelling capability is a fast orthogonal image viewer with activation layers, thresholding, colormaps, linked coordinates, and ROI selection. Its larger purpose is to let a researcher move from seeing a pattern to interrogating it, revising the analysis, and examining the new result without leaving the workspace.

The product composes two existing foundations: ScalaFIM supplies typed neuroimaging data, computations, workflows, provenance, and renderer-neutral graphics; ScalaDock supplies the persistent, rearrangeable JavaFX workspace. The workbench supplies the interaction language between data, selections, queries, views, and analysis actions.

Founding product decision. The orthogonal viewer is the visual center of gravity, but it is not the architectural center. The architectural center is a typed graph of immutable scientific resources connected by selections, derivations, and lawful workspace transitions.

Vision and thesis

Canonical vision

ScalaFIM Workbench is where ScalaFIM becomes explorable. It is an interactive scientific environment in which fMRI data, spatial selections, models, analyses, and results remain connected, so researchers can move fluidly from seeing a pattern, to interrogating it, to changing the analysis that produced it—without sacrificing spatial correctness or computational provenance.
ObserveSelectAskComputeCompareRefine

The workbench should feel like an IDE for functional imaging: persistent panes, explicit scientific objects, contextual commands, background tasks, linked views, inspectable problems, and a workspace that can be saved and resumed. It is not a collection of unrelated utilities arranged in tabs. Every feature participates in a common conversation through resources and selections.

Inspect

Images, activation maps, designs, metadata, diagnostics, and provenance remain visible and navigable.

Explore

Linked views, ROIs, time courses, plots, comparisons, and subject subsets turn observations into questions.

Act

Typed analysis plans can be constructed, preflighted, launched, monitored, and revisited in the same workspace.

Role of the viewer

The orthogonal viewer is the visual center of gravity, but the product is not merely a viewer. Its architectural center is the typed relationship among scientific resources, selections, queries, derivations, and views. A spatial selection made in the viewer can therefore drive FIR plots, group summaries, atlas queries, connectivity analyses, exports, or a revised analysis plan.

Product promise

A user can point to something scientifically meaningful and ask the next valid question without manually translating coordinates, filenames, contrasts, ROIs, or provenance between programs.

Problem and opportunity

Context is lost

Image viewers commonly know pixels and filenames but not the model, contrast, statistic, degrees of freedom, or workflow that produced a map.

Selections do not travel

Coordinates and ROIs must be manually recreated when moving from image inspection to plotting, extraction, group summaries, or new analyses.

Execution is detached

Analysis launchers tend to be form front ends. They rarely preserve the exploratory question that motivated the analysis or ingest results back into context.

ScalaFIM creates an opportunity to replace file-oriented interaction with a typed scientific object model. ScalaDock creates an opportunity to let those objects remain visible in a researcher-defined workspace. The workbench should exploit both without pushing UI concerns into ScalaFIM or neuroimaging concerns into ScalaDock.

Users and jobs

UserPrimary jobsWhat success feels like
fMRI researcher Explore maps, define ROIs, examine FIR/time-course responses, compare subjects and conditions. Questions can be answered interactively without losing analysis context.
Methods developer Inspect designs and fits, compare pipelines, validate outputs, diagnose spatial or statistical discrepancies. Every result is inspectable, typed, and traceable to its producing plan.
Analyst or lab member Open a study, run a validated analysis, monitor tasks, review problems, and hand off a reproducible workspace. The software guides valid work without hiding scientific choices.
Feature author Add a pane, command, query, inspector, or result adapter without changing the workbench kernel. A new feature integrates through small typed contribution points.

Canonical job story

When I notice an activation pattern, I want to select the relevant region, examine its response across conditions and subjects, inspect the model and provenance that produced it, and optionally launch a revised analysis, so that exploration and computation form one reproducible chain of reasoning.

Product principles

  1. Exploration first. Existing data and results are valuable without launching an analysis.
  2. Scientific objects, not anonymous files. Files are boundary representations of typed resources.
  3. Selections are first-class. Points, ROIs, parcels, subjects, conditions, and contrasts can move between features.
  4. Provenance is visible. Users can inspect what produced a result and what assumptions govern its display.
  5. Invalid states are difficult to construct. ADTs, opaque types, smart constructors, and witnesses carry invariants.
  6. Functional core, imperative shell. State transitions and render planning are pure; IO and JavaFX are interpreters.
  7. Modular by composition. Features contribute typed capabilities without becoming dependencies of the kernel.
  8. Responsiveness is correctness. Long computation never owns the JavaFX thread; stale results never overwrite current state.

Scope and non-goals

Initial product scope

  • Persistent JavaFX docking workspace using ScalaDock.
  • Orthogonal volume viewing with linked world coordinates.
  • Anatomical, statistic, and label layers with typed display policies.
  • Activation thresholding, display ranges, opacity, interpolation, and colormaps.
  • Point, ROI, parcel, and cluster selection.
  • FIR/time-course exploration over selected regions and subject groups.
  • Renderer-neutral plots rendered through the ScalaFIM JavaFX backend.
  • Study/result explorer, analysis preflight, task monitoring, and result ingestion.
  • Workspace, layout, and preference persistence.

Explicit non-goals for the founding release

  • A public runtime plugin marketplace or arbitrary dynamic class loading.
  • A complete replacement for every established neuroimaging viewer.
  • Distributed execution as the application foundation.
  • Silent spatial registration or filename-based inference of compatibility.
  • A generic node-graph workflow editor before concrete analysis journeys are proven.
  • Embedding scientific arrays, JavaFX nodes, open readers, or futures in persisted workspace state.
  • Moving UI-specific concepts into ScalaFIM core modules.

Core experience

Default workspace

┌────────────────┬────────────────────────────────┬────────────────┐
│ Study/results  │ Orthogonal viewer              │ Layers         │
│ explorer       │                                │ Inspector      │
├────────────────┴────────────────────────────────┴────────────────┤
│ Plots / Tasks / Problems / Console                               │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

The global menu, command palette, toolbar, status bar, and notifications belong to the shell. Scientific tools and documents belong in dockable panes. Panes may be tabbed, split, maximized, minimized, or moved into other windows without altering scientific state.

Primary exploration journey

  1. Open an anatomical image and one or more activation maps from a result bundle.
  2. Navigate sagittal, coronal, and axial views around one shared world-coordinate cursor.
  3. Adjust activation threshold, colormap, display range, opacity, and layer order.
  4. Select a cluster, atlas parcel, or custom ROI in the reference spatial domain.
  5. Invoke a context-valid FIR response query for chosen conditions and subjects.
  6. Inspect the resulting curve, uncertainty summary, query definition, and provenance.
  7. Inspect the producing analysis specification and preflight a modified analysis.
  8. Launch the plan, monitor progress, and ingest new results into the existing workspace.

Typed product algebra

The workbench is modeled as a typed graph of immutable resources, connected by derivations and explored through domain-anchored selections. Views are projections; interaction is a deterministic algebra of state transitions. 

Resource[A] ── Derivation[A, B] ──▶ Resource[B]
    │                                      │
Selection[A]                          ViewSpec[B]
    │                                      │
    └────────── Query[A, Q] ──▶ Observation[Q]

Core families

final case class ResourceRef[A] private (
    id: ResourceId,
    tpe: ResourceType[A],
    revision: ResourceRevision
)

trait Derivation[I, O]:
  def id: OperationId
  def preflight(input: I): Either[DerivationError, Validated[I]]
  def describe(input: Validated[I]): DerivationPlan[O]

final case class Selection[D, A] private (
    domain: DomainRef[D],
    value: A
)

trait ViewSpec[A]:
  type State
  def initial(resource: ResourceRef[A]): State
  def present(resource: ResourceSnapshot[A], state: State): Presentation

trait Feature:
  type State
  type Intent
  type Effect
  def reduce(state: State, intent: Intent):
    Either[FeatureError, Transition[State, Effect]]

Layer algebra

enum ImageLayer:
  case Anatomy(id: LayerId, source: VolumeRef[Anatomy], style: AnatomyStyle)
  case Statistic(id: LayerId, source: VolumeRef[StatisticField], style: StatisticStyle)
  case Labels(id: LayerId, source: VolumeRef[LabelField], style: LabelStyle)

enum DisplayThreshold:
  case Unthresholded
  case Positive(cutoff: ThresholdCutoff)
  case Negative(cutoff: ThresholdCutoff)
  case TwoSided(magnitude: ThresholdCutoff)
  case Outside(interval: DisplayInterval)

Arbitrary file-discovered facts such as affine compatibility should not be pretended into compile-time constants. Smart constructors produce runtime witnesses such as SameGrid or Alignment, and later operations require those witnesses. The type system carries the proof after the trust boundary.

Laws and invariants

Laws are executable product requirements. Compiler-enforced distinctions, constructor validation, property tests, numerical fixtures, and scenario tests collectively form the proof surface.

Derivation identityA no-op derivation changes neither content nor provenance.identity >>> f = f = f >>> identity
Derivation associativityComposition grouping cannot change the described scientific operation.(f >>> g) >>> h = f >>> (g >>> h)
Validity preservationA successful reducer transition always produces another valid state.valid(s) ∧ reduce(s,i)=Right(t) ⇒ valid(t.state)
Failure atomicityAn invalid intent changes no state and emits no effects.reduce(s, invalid) = Left(error)
ReplayIntent sequences compose by ordinary fold.fold(s, xs ++ ys) = fold(fold(s,xs),ys)
Stale-result exclusionAn asynchronous result for an older revision cannot overwrite current state.completion.revision < current.revision ⇒ unchanged
Layout independenceDock edits commute with disjoint scientific edits.dock ∘ science = science ∘ dock
Presentation invarianceZoom, colormap, threshold display, and docking do not mutate source content or provenance.resource(before) = resource(after)
Spatial round tripValid affine conversion returns the original location within tolerance.voxelToWorld(worldToVoxel(p)) ≈ p
Selection transportTransport respects identity and transform composition.transport(g∘f,s) = transport(g,transport(f,s))
Threshold monotonicityRaising a magnitude cutoff cannot add visible samples.b ≥ a ⇒ support(Tb(x)) ⊆ support(Ta(x))
Threshold idempotenceApplying the same threshold twice is the same as applying it once.T(T(x)) = T(x)
Color-support independenceChanging a colormap cannot change the threshold support.support(C1(x)) = support(C2(x))
Alpha identitiesTransparent content is the compositing identity; opaque over transparent preserves content.transparent over x = x
Linked-cursor coherenceAll compatible linked views converge on the same world point.cursor(v₁) = cursor(v₂) = worldPoint
Immutable identityThe same resource identity and revision denote the same scientific content.same(id,revision) ⇒ same(content)
Provenance completenessEvery derived resource names its inputs, operation version, parameters, and warnings.derived(r) ⇒ provenance(r).inputs.nonEmpty
Command applicabilityA contextual command is enabled exactly when its typed context can be constructed.enabled(c,w) ⇔ c.context(w).isDefined

Architecture and ownership

Application
workbench-app — composition root, feature installation, toolchain, runtime configuration, and JavaFX lifecycle.
UI shell
workbench-javafx — ScalaDock panes, controls, canvases, command palette, task center, and UI-thread interpretation.
JVM runtime
workbench-runtime-jvm — resource loading, caches, background tasks, filesystem/result watching, cancellation, and analysis runners.
Features
imaging · exploration · analysis — pure state, intents, effects, queries, plans, render requests, and domain adapters.
Kernel
workbench-kernel — typed resources, selections, commands, jobs, notifications, transitions, feature contribution contracts, and persistence descriptors.
Foundations
ScalaFIM owns scientific computation and graphics. ScalaDock owns docking layout and pane lifecycle. Neither depends on the workbench.

Repository boundary

The preferred implementation is a third repository, provisionally scalafim-workbench. This PRD lives in ScalaFIM while the product is being defined because ScalaFIM owns the scientific architecture it consumes. Once a workbench repository is created, the canonical PRD should move there and ScalaFIM should link to it.

Compilation and sampling are performed off the JavaFX thread. Only the final draw touches JavaFX. Render cache keys include resource revision, spatial reference, plane, cursor, viewport dimensions, layer order, display policy, and interpolation.

Provisional performance targets

  • Cached cursor/slice updates become visible within 100 ms at p95 on the reference development machine.
  • Typical uncached local slice updates become visible within 250 ms at p95.
  • Rapid cursor movement remains interactive through cancellation/coalescing and stale-result exclusion.
  • Opening or restoring a workspace reports progress rather than blocking the JavaFX thread.
  • Cache use is bounded by an explicit policy and observable in diagnostics.

Targets must be calibrated against representative datasets during Phase 1; they are not release claims yet.

Evolutionary delivery plan

PhaseOutcomeAcceptance gate
0 — Compatibility spike A minimal application consumes ScalaFIM and ScalaDock and renders a synthetic ScalaFIM raster in a docked JavaFX pane. One verified Scala/OpenJFX matrix; lifecycle, packaging, and headless-test strategy recorded.
1 — Viewer skeleton One anatomical volume, three linked planes, world cursor, zoom/pan, coordinate readout, workspace save/restore. Orientation phantom, affine round-trip, stale-render, and layout-independence scenarios pass.
2 — Activation layers Statistic and label layers, ordering, thresholding, colormaps, range, opacity, interpolation, and alignment checks. Threshold laws, independent pixel compositing oracles, and incompatible-space rejection pass.
3 — ROI and FIR exploration Domain-anchored ROI selections, subject/condition filters, group FIR summaries, and docked plots. Fixture-backed ROI aggregation and full selection-to-plot provenance scenario pass.
4 — Analysis workbench Study explorer, spec editor, preflight, local runner, task center, result ingestion, and provenance navigation. Invalid plans cannot launch; cancellation and result identity scenarios pass.
5 — Feature platform Reference features for design diagnostics, QC, connectivity, or multivariate results use stable contribution contracts. A third feature integrates without kernel modification; extension API and compatibility policy are documented.
Release discipline. Each phase is a complete vertical slice with a realistic workflow contract. A broad pane inventory is not progress if the central observe–select–ask loop remains unproven.

Success measures

Founding-release outcomes

  • A researcher can open a study result, inspect spatially aligned layers, select an ROI, and obtain a provenance-bearing FIR plot in one workspace.
  • No workflow step requires manual transfer of world coordinates, voxel indices, ROI files, or contrast names between tools.
  • Every displayed scientific result can navigate to its resource metadata and producing operation or explicitly report that provenance is unavailable.
  • Closing, docking, floating, or restoring panes never changes scientific state.
  • An invalid spatial overlay or invalid analysis plan is rejected before execution or display.
  • Feature authors can add a representative new inspector or plot feature without modifying the workbench kernel.

Product telemetry without surveillance

Early evaluation should rely on opt-in local diagnostics and structured usability sessions, not remote behavioral telemetry by default. Useful local measures include render latency, cache hit rate, task duration, cancellation rate, problem categories, and time from selection to observation. Any future telemetry requires explicit consent and must exclude image data, paths, subject identifiers, coordinates, and analysis payloads.

Open product questions

  • Which ROI creation modes belong in the first exploration release: atlas parcel, thresholded cluster, brush, polygon, or all four?
  • Should multiple viewers link cursor, zoom, slice plane, ROI, and layer style independently through separate channels?
  • What is the canonical persisted representation of a derived ROI when its source result is revised?
  • Which FIR/group uncertainty summaries are scientifically required for the first real study workflow?
  • Should analysis execution initially be in-process, an isolated local worker process, or both?
  • What packaging strategy provides reliable JavaFX natives across macOS, Linux, and Windows?
