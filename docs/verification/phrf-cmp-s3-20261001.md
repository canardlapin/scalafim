## !! SamplingFrame defaults the scan start to TR/2

`SamplingFrame(blockLens, tr)` with no `startTime` places scan k at `(k + 1/2) * TR`. The generator places sample k of a run at `k * TR` (`SCHEMA.md`: `sample_time = n*TR`). Any PHRF fit built with the default frame sees the data 0.5 s late. The PHRF arm here passes `startTime` from the run's first `sample_time` and refuses (typed `Setup(frame, IrregularSampling)`) if a run is not `start + i * tr`.

Effect on earlier work:
- **S11 fixtures: not affected.** The R side set `start_time = 0` explicitly (S11 receipt, gotcha 4), and the Scala parity here uses scan times `k * TR` throughout.
- **S0 receipt: affected in its numbers, not in its structure.** `PhrfSpikeSuite.frameOf` builds `SamplingFrame(blockLens, tr)` with the default, so S0's PHRF fits (33 Accepted / 7 Boundary in C-TX-.5, 32/5/2/1 in T-TX-fast, the median within-voxel correlations 0.993 and 0.901, and the fitted `tau`) were computed on a half-TR-shifted model. S0's conclusions about callability (route names, refusals, `fromFixed` refusing AR, admission-after-rho, preparation and run timings, work receipts) do not depend on the shift. Its Accepted/Boundary rates and accuracy previews should not be quoted; the 17.5 % and 20 % non-Accepted fractions in the design (section 7 risk 8) are not reliable. Not re-measured here.

### Typed refusal and failure capture

- Rank deficiency, native arms: a condition with no events (zero design columns) and two conditions with identical events both give `failed:fit.RankDeficientDesign` for CAN, INF3 and FIR. FIR with 8 events per run (24 events against 96 bins plus 18 nuisance columns) is genuinely rank deficient: `failed:fit.RankDeficientDesign`, while CAN and INF3 estimate on the same data (locked as its own test). With 16 events per run of 200 scans, all three estimate.
- PHRF with an empty condition returns a typed outcome (not Estimated).
- `Boundary`: a Gaussian with tau = 20 s, noise-free, decodes with tau pinned at the chart upper bound 8 and gives `refused:decode.Boundary`; the adjacent in-chart voxel stays `Estimated`. Justification from the definition, not a probe: the Boundary rule is `ShapeDecoder.scala:325-330` (`onBoundary`, applied at `:614`): a decoded coordinate `x_i` with `x_i <= lower_i + 1e-12` or `x_i >= upper_i - 1e-12`. The Gaussian chart is tau in [3, 8] s (`GaussianFamily.scala:107`). Boundary is a property of the decoded point, not of the truth. tau = 11 lies outside the chart but is not a boundary case: the free log-sd axis lets an interior point fit it (observed decode tau 5.38, status Accepted). tau = 20 puts the response beyond the 24 s span, so the best chart point pins tau at 8. The design (section 2.1) does not say tau = 11 should be a Boundary case.
- Mismatched cell kind (`failed:kind-mismatch`), voxel-count mismatch (`failed:inconsistent`).
- PHRF `DecodeStatus` other than `Accepted` (including `WeaklyIdentified`) is a `Refused`; dataset-level PHRF stage errors are `Refused(DatasetLevel(stage, constructorName))`; harness setup errors are `Failed`.

## Owner decision recorded: FIR E-resp is piecewise constant (2026-10-01)

Design 2.1 originally said FIR bins are taken to 0.1 s by piecewise-linear interpolation through bin centres. The owner decided on 2026-10-01 that FIR E-resp is **piecewise constant** (the response the FIR model actually estimates, equal to fmrireg `fitted_hrf`, matching the fixtures at 1e-8; the pre-registered rule with no secondary). The design paragraph (2.1, FIR row) is amended with a dated note. The linear option was removed from the code; `KernelBasis.Fir(bins, binWidth)` is the only form, constant on `[k w, (k+1) w)` and zero from the last edge on (including at exactly H).

## Ambiguities resolved

- Peak normalisation: CAN uses the raw SPMG1 kernel (fixtures and library), E-resp is scale invariant. The protocol's "peak-normalized" wording was not applied to betas.
- `ArmStatus` was renamed `ConditionArmStatus` and `VoxelOutcome` to `ConditionVoxel` to stay clear of S4's `TrialArmStatus` and S8's `score.VoxelOutcome`. `NotRun` maps to `Failed` in the S8 adapter (guard, not a path the pilot takes).
- The scorer's MISE (`ConditionAdapter.mise`) is the trapezoid rule on the shared 0.1 s grid, averaged over conditions. Building the true response curves from `ScoreTruth` is the scorer's job; the adapter takes them as input.
- `phrfComparison` previously did not depend on `fit`; the `build.sbt` line was changed. The `SamplingFrame` start-time finding above applies to anyone else building frames for the generator data.

## F1 (fairness defect): off-grid onsets, fixed by lowering at 0.1 s

How the compact route handles onsets: `term.convolve(basis.kernel, frame, precision = 0.2)` (via `Regressor.evalConv`, `buildDriveMeasure`) does not snap and is not exact. It splits each impulse linearly (hat basis) between the two surrounding lowering nodes, convolves with the kernel sampled on that grid, and reads the result at the scans. That is exact for an onset on a node and has an O(h^2) interpolation error otherwise. The exact-lag native arms do not pay it, so at a 0.2 s grid PHRF carried a systematic handicap whenever an onset was an odd multiple of 0.1 s (the generator puts onsets on a 0.1 s grid).

Measured, noise-free, three Gaussian voxels, max |E-resp - direct| / peak response (kernels that are about 1e-7 at lag 0, like a real HRF):

| Onsets (first, gap), TR | lowering 0.2 s | lowering 0.1 s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| (4.0, 11.2), TR 1 (on the 0.2 grid) | 1.6e-5 (original truths) | 3.2e-6 |
| (4.1, 11.3), TR 1 | 3.2e-3 | 2.5e-6 |
| (4.1, 11.1), TR 1 | 3.2e-3 | 3.6e-6 |
| (4.0, 11.2), TR 0.5 | 2.1e-3 (original truths) | 1.2e-6 |
| (4.1, 11.3), TR 0.5 | 6.3e-3 (original truths) | 1.2e-6 |

The sd error shows the same split (about 3e-3 at 0.2 s, about 1e-6 at 0.1 s). The default `PhrfConditionConfig.loweringSeconds` is now 0.1; the 1e-4 test bound is unchanged and all five off-grid and TR cases meet it with margin; a contrast test pins that 0.2 s is over 100 times worse. The ".5" in the cell id `C-TX-.5` is the SNR, not the TR; the pilot condition cells all have TR 1.0. TR 0.5 is tested as a stress case only. **S5 and any other PHRF user that lowers on a 0.2 s grid inherits the same defect** (T-TS cells have 0.1 s onsets).

Cost of the finer grid, timed cheaply on the synthetic case (3 runs x 100 scans, 24 events, 3 voxels, one JVM, warm): kernel-basis compile 0.31 s at 0.2 s versus 1.59 s at 0.1 s (once per `PhrfConditionConfig` instance; the defaults are now one shared lazy instance, so it is paid once per process); `run` including `prepare` 0.11-0.13 s at 0.2 s versus 0.13-0.16 s at 0.1 s (one 0.49 s outlier, JIT noise). Not measured at pilot size.

**Lag-0 residual (closed, revision 3, 2026-10-02): a test-generator artifact, not a convention mismatch.** The 2.9e-4-of-peak residual (original truths, onsets first 4.1, gap 11.3) was caused by `ConditionSynth` computing onsets as `first + gap * k`, which gives 4.1 + 33.9 = 38.00000000000001, one ulp after the scan at 38.0. The truth then saw lag = -1.4e-14 (causal, so zero) while PHRF's linear impulse split puts the onset on the node and counts the lag-0 sample in full. Isolating it: weighting the truth's lag-0 sample 1 / 0.5 / 0 gave an identical 1.9e-4 and 2.9e-4 in all three cases before the fix (the coincidence was not exact, so the weight never applied), and 2.1e-6 / 9.5e-5 / 1.9e-4 (voxel 1) after onsets were rounded to the decimal. After the fix (onsets are the nearest double to the 0.1 s decimal, as the generator's `k / 10.0`) the original truths give 2.1e-6, 6.9e-6 and 7.1e-6.

Conventions at lag exactly 0 (an onset on a scan), who matches whom: the generator counts the lag-0 sample in full (`kernels.py:38` `m = t >= 0`; `generate.py:116-123` response `f(lag) / pk`); the native arms' exact-lag `Regressor` counts it in full (`lag >= 0`, `Regressor.evalLoop`; confirmed to 1e-13 against fmrireg on the S11 fixtures); the PHRF compact route counts it in full (the impulse on a lowering node, fine kernel starting at lag 0). All three agree; there is no code to change in any slice. T-TX-like check (every onset exactly on a scan, TR 1, truths at the chart edges where the lag-0 density is largest: tau 3.1 / sd 0.85, tau 7.8 / sd 0.85, tau 3.2 / sd 1.0): max |E-resp - direct| / peak 1.1e-5 (per voxel 1.1e-5, 1.1e-5, 3.2e-6; tau errors at most 7e-6), the same as the off-scan control (2.0e-5). The convention is not vacuous: a truth that counts the lag-0 sample at half weight or not at all gives 6.1e-4 and 1.2e-3 on the same design (tested, bound 1e-4). Original truths with every onset on a scan: 4.8e-6, 7.7e-6, 2.0e-5.

## F3: input binding

`ConditionRunner.inputsBound` replaces the y-only check. Bit for bit (no tolerance), on this machine only (no cross-platform claim for any hash): the whitened `y`; the nuisance intercept split; and the pre-fit design (a function of onsets, conditions, runs, sample times and nuisance, whitened with the shared plan) against `prep.whitened.prefitDesign`. One-bit-visible changes to nuisance, a whole-bin change of one onset, one condition label, or a whole-bin change of one sample time are each refused by all four arms (`failed:inputs-not-prepared`). **Residual gap (closed in revision 4, 2026-10-02):** the pre-fit design resolves onsets and sample times to its 1 s FIR bins, so a sub-bin change of an onset or a scan time was not detected, because `CommonPrep` kept no raw or hashed copy of the events or sample times. S2 then added an exact SHA-256 input fingerprint (`54994dbb`: onsets, conditions, durations, sample times, run ids, event runs, nuisance and y); `inputsBound` now calls `CommonPrep.requireMatches` first and keeps the three independent checks. A 0.1 s shift of one onset or one scan time inside the same 1 s bin is now refused by all four arms (`failed:inputs-not-prepared`; new test). The test-only noise-free prep helper updates the fingerprint along with the recomputed whitened response.

PHRF's internal whitening: the response block is whitened through `WhiteningTransform.matrix` with the plan inside `CanonicalTemporalWhitening.Shared` (the same plan object as `prep.plan`, asserted with `eq`); S2's arrays use `Whiten.series` on the same plan. The test replicates the executor's call on the same raw data and compares IEEE bits: 0 bits of difference (both JVM and JS).

## F4 (owner decision 2026-10-02): CAN and INF3 E-resp truncated at 24 s

Each arm is scored on exactly what its fitted design represents: the SPM E-resp is exactly zero after 24 s (the span of the design kernels; 24 s itself is kept, as in the convolution). fmrireg parity of E-resp is asserted on 0-24 s only (the whole grid for FIR); beyond 24 s our curves are asserted to be exactly zero. The 24-32 s difference from fmrireg `fitted_hrf` is a documented deliberate deviation. Design 2.1 carries a dated note. The `S11` parity numbers in the table above for CAN and INF3 are therefore on 0-24 s from this revision; all stay at or below 1.8e-13.
