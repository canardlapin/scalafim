## Amendment (owner decision): type-B parity against a frozen float32 bound

The original verdict above (literal 1e-6 FAIL) is retained unchanged as the record of the first run. The owner then decided (1) to replace the 1e-6 criterion for type B by a bound derived from float32 precision, and (2) to put the T-G cells on TR-aligned onsets (the generator agent's task; resolves limitation 2 once done, not verified here).

**Amended verdict: PASS** (type B noise-free parity within the frozen bound at baselines 1, 100, 1000; negative controls separate by 2 to 3.7 orders of magnitude). Types C/D, units, normalization and runtime results are unchanged. Overall gate: PASS under the amended criterion; the literal-1e-6 FAIL stands as history.

**Criterion change (explicit).** The original criterion was elementwise/per-entry relative error <= 1e-6 against truth; the amended criterion is per-voxel relative L2 error of the trial-beta vector against a float64 fit of the same model, within the frozen bound. These are different metrics (the elementwise worst case, 6.1e-5 at baseline 100, is harsher than the L2 value 9.1e-6).

**Comparison target.** Not the truth: a float64 fit of exactly the same model (same single-trial design on the volume grid, same HRF = GLMsingle's returned library column, same polynomial projection `maxpolydeg=1`), converted to psc with GLMsingle's own `meanvol`. Metric: per-voxel relative L2 error of the 144 trial betas in psc units.

**Solver actually used** (`ols/fit_model.py:230` calling `ols/olsmatrix2.py`): the projected design X = P D(h) is held in float32 (probed: float32, 600 x 144); `X.T @ X` is formed in float32, promoted to float64 by `+ lambda*I`, and solved as `solve(X'X, X')` (**normal equations, no column normalization**, so conditioning enters as kappa^2); then `betas = f @ data` with float32 data (`mtimes_stack`), cast to float32 (`glm_estimatemodel.py:790`); psc = beta*100/|meanvol| with float32 `meanvol` (`:523`, `:945-960`).

**Frozen bound** (`precision_bound.py`, `results/precision_bound_frozen.json`, committed as 0349a1d **before** the rerun; the form and c = 1 were chosen after the first-run diagnosis (float32 cast, kappa, baseline dependence) and frozen before the type-B rerun; they were not tuned afterwards, and are confirmed on unseen designs below):

```
E <= c * [ kappa^2 * (sqrt(n) + 2 sqrt(p)) * u        float32 Gram X'X (random-rounding model) + float32 rounding of X
         + kappa * u * ||d||2 / ||X b64||2            float32 data/projection; d = raw data incl. baseline
         + (log2(n) + 4) * u ]                        float32 meanvol (pairwise), 100/meanvol, float32 casts
u = 2^-24 = 5.96e-8, c = 1, n = 600 volumes, p = 144 trials, kappa = cond2(X) for the voxel's HRF
```

kappa(X) from the design alone, over the 20 library HRFs: 3.97 to 7.96 (frozen table in the JSON); recomputed at test time and equal to the frozen values (relative difference 0). `||d||2/||X b64||2` is the data-magnitude ratio per voxel (baseline-dependent) computed from the data and the float64 reference, not from truth.

The elementwise worst case of 6.1e-5 (baseline 100) is a different, harsher metric than the L2 metric the bound is stated for.

**Negative controls** (the same GLMsingle output compared against a deliberately wrong float64 reference model; minimum over voxels of error / bound):

| Control | Min error | Min error / bound (b=1, 100, 1000) |
|---|---|---|

| 1-TR onset shift | 0.482 | 2880, 2316, 772 |

The bound is not vacuous: wrong models exceed it by a factor of at least 175 (more than 2 orders), and it accepts only float32-level disagreement. Caveat: the bound is conservative for small baselines (the kappa^2 sqrt(n) term dominates, about 60-90x margin) and tightest at baseline 1000 (5.2x), so it should not be reused for much larger baselines, longer runs or worse-conditioned (denser) designs without recomputing from the formula (which takes only the design, n, p and the data norm ratio). It is a derived worst-case-style estimate with c = 1, not a proof.

**Bound notes.** (i) The meanvol term is conservative: the float64 reference uses GLMsingle's own float32 meanvol, so meanvol rounding cancels in the comparison and only the casts remain. (ii) The data projection `P @ d` runs in float32 (`glm_estimatemodel.py:560-562`); it is covered by the data term (absolute error about u*||d||).

## Source-verified facts (file:line at the pinned commit; paths under `glmsingle/`)

**Beta units (percent signal change, not raw).**
- `defaults.py:22` `wantpercentbold = 1`.
- `ols/glm_estimatemodel.py:523` `meanvol = concatenate(data, axis=-1).mean(axis=-1)`: the per-voxel mean over all volumes of all runs, including the signal.
- `ols/glm_estimatemodel.py:945-960`: `betasmd *= 100 / |meanvol|`. Doc: `glmsingle.py:184-188`.
- Types C and D are recomputed with `wantpercentbold = 0` internally (`glmsingle.py:1342, 1534`) and converted once at the end: `glmsingle.py:1639` `modelmd = modelmd / |meanvol| * 100`.
- Raw data units are obtainable with `wantpercentbold = 0` (checked numerically).

**Library HRF normalization (peak = 1 per HRF).**
- `hrf/gethrf.py:94` normalizes the 20-HRF library by its **global** maximum (so raw library peaks range 0.349 to 1.0; measured). The docstring at `gethrf.py:54-55` claiming a per-HRF maximum of one is wrong for that function alone.
- `glmsingle.py:474` then `normalisemax(hrflibrary, 0)`: per-column (per-HRF) maximum = 1. `glmsingle.py:469` does the same for `hrftoassume` (global). The fit regressors therefore have peak exactly 1 on the TR-sampled grid.
- Consequence: a response `a * h_raw` is fitted as `beta = a * peak(h_raw)`, in data units, before the psc conversion.

**Outputs of types A-D** (memory outputs `results['typea'..'typed']`).
- A: `glmsingle.py:928-931`: `onoffR2`, `meanvol`, `betasmd`. One ON-OFF beta per voxel (all trials one regressor, canonical HRF `hrftoassume`). HRF is not fitted.
- B: `glmsingle.py:1141-1146, 1220`: `HRFindex`, `HRFindexrun`, `R2`, `FitHRFR2`, `betasmd` (V x n_trials), `meanvol`. One HRF per voxel, argmax of R2 over the library (`glmsingle.py:1035`); one regressor per trial (`designSINGLE`, `glmsingle.py:583-612`), so these are joint single-trial (LSA-style) betas.
- C: `glmsingle.py:1788-1800`: adds `pcnum`, `noisepool`, `pcregressors`. D: `glmsingle.py:1803-1818`: adds `FRACvalue`, `scaleoffset`. C and D `betasmd` are V x n_trials.
- Trial order is chronological (`glmsingle.py:368`). The design is **volume-resolution** (time x conditions, binary): onsets are on the TR grid; two trials in one volume are an error (`glmsingle.py:597-612`).

**Defaults** (`defaults.py`):
- GLMdenoise: `wantglmdenoise=1` (l.15), `n_pcs=10` (l.11), `pcstop=1.05` (l.29; number of PCs chosen by cross-validation, folds = runs, `glmsingle.py:438-439`), `brainthresh=[99, 0.1]` (l.24). `pcR2cutoff` and `brainR2` empty, so set from a GMM tail threshold on ON-OFF R2 (`glmsingle.py:936-947`). Noise pool = bright voxels with ON-OFF R2 below that threshold (`glmsingle.py:1231-1247`). The pool is **data-selected**, not specified by the caller.
- fracridge: `wantfracridge=1` (l.16), `fracs = linspace(1, 0.05, 20)` (l.30), `wantautoscale=1` (l.31). Fraction chosen per voxel by cross-validation across runs.
- Also: `wantlibrary=1` (l.14), `wantlss=0` (l.23), `seed=time.time()` (l.32; the wrapper sets it explicitly), `chunklen=50000`.

**Float32** (the cause of the parity failure): data cast at `check_inputs.py:53` and `ols/glm_estimatemodel.py:434`; design at `check_inputs.py:69`; betas at `ols/glm_estimatemodel.py:790`.

**Non-degenerate input is required, even for type B.** `gmm/findtailthreshold.py:55` (called unconditionally at `glmsingle.py:936-940`) fits a two-component GMM with `reg_covar=0` to ON-OFF R2. A dataset of noise-free voxels only makes it raise ValueError. The protocol's datasets (with noise-pool voxels) are fine; noise-free parity has to include noisy pool voxels (done here).

## Units: conversion formula (GLMsingle units to data units)

```
beta_data(v, trial) = beta_psc(v, trial) / 100 * |meanvol(v)| * peak(h_used(v))
E-trial             = beta_data            (peak(h_used) = 1 for the library GLMsingle actually uses)
```

- `meanvol(v)` is the returned `meanvol`: the mean over all volumes of the **data given** (signal plus baseline). It is not the declared baseline 100: in the test data it ranged 99.77 to 100.21; substituting 100 biases betas by up to 0.23 %.
- `peak(h_used)` is 1 by the normalization above, on the TR-sampled grid. If a generator defines E-trial as the peak of the continuous response, and the true peak falls between samples, E-trial exceeds the sampled peak; the runner must account for that (and for onset rounding, below).
- Direction: estimated data-unit amplitude of a response `a * h_raw` is `a * peak(h_raw)`.
- The wrapper returns both `*_beta_psc` and `*_beta_data` (`run_glmsingle.py`).

Numeric confirmation (`results/units_and_norm.json`, noise-free scored voxels plus 400 noise voxels, baseline 100, 4 runs x 36 stimuli, 150 volumes, TR 1, stimdur 1):
- Library peaks: raw 0.349-1.0; as used 1.0 (exact).
- psc = beta_data / meanvol * 100 to 6.1e-5 (float32 floor); `wantpercentbold=0` output equals raw data units.
- Type A (equal positive amplitudes, canonical HRF): data-unit error 2.4e-7, psc 2.4e-7.
- `meanvol` equals the float64 mean of the data to 1.1e-5 absolute.

## Wrapper

`tools/phrf-comparison/glmsingle/run_glmsingle.py`: documented schema in the module docstring; Python function `run_glmsingle(...)` and CLI (`--input/--output npz`, `--seed` required, `--tr`, `--stimdur`, `--threads`). Pure function of its inputs: seed passed in params, no globals written, no files written (`wantfileoutputs = [0,0,0,0]`, `outputdir=None`, `figuredir=None`). GLMsingle's only use of the global numpy RNG is a figure-jitter that is disabled. Outputs are chronological; `trial_order` maps back to input order. Onsets are rounded to the nearest volume (half up); the maximum shift is reported in `meta` and was 0.5 s for sub-TR test onsets.

## Limitations and cautions for the runner

1. **Parity FAIL** as above; float32 floor about 1e-5 at baseline 100 (about 1e-4 at baseline 1000). Decision needed on the 1e-6 tolerance.
2. **Onset quantization.** GLMsingle takes TR-resolution designs. The protocol's 0.1 s onset grid (sub-TR onsets) is rounded by the wrapper, up to 0.5 s at TR 1. This is a systematic design mismatch between GLMsingle and arms that use true onsets; truth for T-G should be defined consistently (for example, scoring against amplitudes generated at the true onsets, which include the jitter as extra error). Needs a protocol decision; I did not test a sub-TR-onset recovery.
3. **Type D shrinkage.** D's cross-validation assumes repeatable condition responses across runs. Under the protocol's stimulus-persistent or trial-deviating amplitudes, D shrinks toward zero (slope 0.71 in the independent-amplitude test), so its E-trial MSE/deviation/shrinkage slope will reflect this. The scale-free primary (within-condition correlation) is not affected by global scaling. Also `autoscale` is on by default.
4. **Noise-pool composition.** The pool is chosen by GLMsingle from ON-OFF R2; with random-sign condition amplitudes, about 40 % of scored signal voxels joined it in the test. The "4 000 noise-pool voxels" are a generated count, not the realized pool size (296-388 of 500 in the small test; about 2 150-2 450 of 4 040 in the timing runs).
5. **Environment outside provenance.** Dependencies are unpinned upstream and very recent (numpy 2.5, scikit-learn 1.9); the lockfile pins them. Results are valid for this lock only. The 1e-6 criterion might behave differently with other BLAS builds.
6. **Shipped-example parity is self-consistency,** since no reference outputs ship.
7. One design tested for parity (36 stimuli x 4 runs, fast). Slow cells not parity-tested (runtime only).
8. Threading: CPU time varies slightly with BLAS threads (10.6 vs 9.2 core-s); report single-thread figures for budgeting.
